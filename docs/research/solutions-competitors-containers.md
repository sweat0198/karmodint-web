# UK competitor research: container conversions, portable cabins and welfare units

**Access date:** 2026-09-16  
**Market:** United Kingdom  
**Sample:** 11 active suppliers with first-party UK evidence  
**Scope:** Shipping-container conversions, portable/anti-vandal cabins, site accommodation, welfare units and adjacent modular products. Storage-only sellers excluded.

## Method and evidence rules

- Official company pages only. No directory, marketplace or press-summary evidence used.
- “Explicit” means company uses label on own current site. “Inference” means analyst grouping, clearly marked.
- Normalized tags use `1` only where first-party page explicitly supports use. `0` means “not established in reviewed evidence”, not “supplier cannot provide it”.
- Delivery model describes reviewed offer: sale, hire, custom conversion, installation or transport.
- Company claims such as “largest” retained only as attributed claims, not independently validated market facts.

## Executive findings

1. **Use-case navigation dominates.** Competitors rarely stop at product types. Most expose offices, welfare/canteens, sanitation, retail/kiosks, classrooms and industrial enclosures as named outcomes.
2. **Hybrid catalogues win breadth.** Strongest competitors combine commodity containers, cabins, modular buildings, custom conversions and delivery/installation. Portable Space, S Jones, Willbox, Algeco and Wernick show this pattern.
3. **Office + welfare is market baseline.** All 11 reviewed firms explicitly show site-office/workspace and canteen/welfare uses. All also show sanitation and storage somewhere in their offer.
4. **Retail is nearly baseline, not niche.** All 11 have explicit retail, reception, ticketing, information-point, kiosk or roadside-retail evidence. Commercial language differs: “catering unit”, “pop-up”, “concession”, “ticket office”, “visitor reception”.
5. **Industrial enclosures bridge container and modular demand.** Every firm has explicit anti-vandal, defence, plant, energy, chemical, workshop or similarly demanding industrial evidence.
6. **Garden/home products form a smaller consumer-facing cluster.** Four firms explicitly market garden rooms/offices. This is distinct from construction-site offices and should remain separate in solution architecture.
7. **Delivery is part of product.** Nationwide depots, HIAB/crane delivery, rapid deployment, relocation, installation and collection recur as conversion benefits. Solution pages should surface deployment method, not bury it under logistics.

5. **Home & Garden** — garden office, garden room, studio, hobby room.
6. **Industrial & Infrastructure** — workshop, plant, switchgear, battery, generator, water treatment, chemical/COSHH.
7. **Health & Care** — screening, vaccination, clinic, field hospital, pharmacy.
8. **Accommodation** — sleeper, staff/worker accommodation, residential/living modules.

## Implications for solution-page design

- Build one page per buyer outcome, then map compatible product families and sizes beneath it.
- Expose **buy / hire / bespoke**, deployment time, delivery region, groundwork/utilities and relocation status near page top.
- Use combined solutions as high-intent subpages: office + storage, office + canteen, canteen + drying, welfare + sanitation.
- Gatehouse page should include access control, guard hut, reception, check-in, ticketing, payments and car-park kiosk terms.
- Keep commercial catering separate from workforce canteen. Same structure; different equipment, regulation and buyer intent.
- Separate garden/home office from site office. Consumer buyer, finish expectations, planning questions and imagery differ.
- Industrial pages need application-led proof: equipment housed, environmental controls, compliance, fire/security, service access and factory acceptance.
- Case studies should state units, size/area, programme, location and delivered fit-out. Lion’s Goodsheds metrics provide strong pattern.
- Every solution page should link to compatible size/configuration records. Competitors often inspire well but leave product fit ambiguous.

## Source-quality cautions

- Fleet sizes, depot counts, customer counts, coverage percentages and superlatives are company claims.
- Company sites change quickly. Revalidate labels before publication or competitive claim use.
- Several suppliers use “site accommodation” for work/welfare buildings. Report counts residential accommodation only where sleeping/living/luxury accommodation appears explicitly.
- Some businesses sell standard containers but custom conversions only; others hire converted/site units. Avoid treating “hire available” as proof every bespoke solution is rentable.
